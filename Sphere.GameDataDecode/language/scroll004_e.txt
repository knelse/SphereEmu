    TO THE NOBLE READER FROM THE AUTHOR
    I am writing this to knowledgeable magicians for help, and to students for the sake of accumulating wisdom.
    
    Those who respect the science of alchemy and are diligent in its study will benefit greatly, those who are not polite to this knowledge will receive rebirth in a bad lot.
    
    Bertrand of Leon, rector of the School of Alchemists, court magician of His Majesty Leon, King of the Sphere. February is the month of the year 1072 from the creation of the Sphere.
    
    ABOUT THE ALCHEMICAL FURNACE
    Without an alchemical furnace, skillfully manufactured and magically processed, it is unthinkable to reach heights in our greatest and subtlest of arts. Everything is subject to the furnace, combining the properties of the four elements, like a living being. And who thinks that there is something beyond her control, he is mistaken in his pride! For there is nothing beyond our control, there is only what we have not discovered. However, an inexperienced magician should not approach an alchemical furnace. One of the greatest magicians Amilus Nomradsky in the year 1008 made an alchemical cauldron, which in low weight and volume stores many of the advantages of a large alchemical furnace. Being small in size, it allows you to carry out, although not all, but many of the most important reactions. This alchemical cauldron should be used by inexperienced students, as well as experienced magicians, when there is no furnace nearby.
    
    TYPES OF ALCHEMICAL REACTIONS
    O noble reader! Without a clear and understandable system to determine the location of each alchemical reaction, it is impossible to comprehend the wisdom of our science. Many pundits have tried to organize those tens and hundreds of recipes that are known to our science. Some ways were witty and can still be useful to this day; others are too confusing and false; others are simply ridiculous. We will not talk about them here, because your humble servant, Bertrand Leonsky, managed to create a clear, understandable and useful classification. It is recognized today by most eminent alchemists.
    So, we divide reactions into three kinds: cardinal, fixed and mutable.
    
    To accomplish a cardinal reaction, three ingredients are needed: a stone, a plant and a Fainal powder. Such reactions are simple, few in number and give exceptionally good or neutral results.
    
    Fixed reactions according to our classification are the development and complication of cardinal ones. They need four ingredients: stone, plant, metal and Amilus powder. Such reactions are capable of giving, in addition to useful, and very dangerous drugs to use, so you should not teach anyone this art.
    By the way, many ignoramuses mistakenly believe that, on the contrary, cardinal reactions are a later simplification of fixed ones, since Fainal, they say, created his powder after Amilus. They are cruelly mistaken, because the latest research in the Shipstone archives convincingly proves that it was reactions with three components that were first used in our Field! Instead of Fainal powder, the root of the Sildarilla plant was used then, which is no longer found under our skies.
    Only five basic metals can be thrown into the alchemical cauldron of Amilus; the alchemical furnace allows the use of other metals in addition to the five basic ones, such as tin, mercury and platinum, which distinguishes these two devices.
    The result of any cardinal or fixed reaction for a reasonable magician is not a non-eventuality: with some skill, it can be guessed by the qualities of the ingredients included in the reaction.
    
    Mutable reactions do not obey any laws; they involve three or four ingredients. Only special powders are required to complete the mutable reaction, while other components can be any, not necessarily even plants and stones. Some even threw their old shoes into the oven and achieved amazing results.
    
    Most pundits consider mutable reactions to be the product of chaos, except for the upstart de Orco, who calls himself Gwydion and is now hiding in the Charonian wilds from the consequences of his own inept magic! However, a promise made on pain of death makes me mention his false theory that every thing in the world has its own inner essence-a name inscribed in an unknown language, the alphabet of this language contains only four letters, and these letters correspond to the four elements: earth, air, water and fire. And so, says de Orco, in mutable reactions, the letters-elements are mixed up, and sometimes they give a meaningful word, so that a known object is born, and sometimes they give nonsense, so that nothing is born or a disaster occurs. But de Orco's theory itself is nothing but nonsense, because he himself could not read a single word that is supposedly hidden in any thing.
